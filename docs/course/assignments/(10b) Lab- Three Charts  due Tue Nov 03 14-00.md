(10b) Lab: Three Charts  due Tue Nov 03 14:00

Charts lab
As we are going to start moving into approaches to visualizing data, for this lab you are going to generate three different charts out of one set of data, then include brief written insights/analysis about the chart conclusion or why it is effective. Note: You CAN just generate them in google sheets (and take screenshots), rather than through scripting.
First, pick a statistical dataset. You could revisit/use ones from previous assignments (college ratings, sports stats, etc), or use some of the health data we have been working with.
Then, paste the data into a new google sheet. (Note: you could also use an excel spreadsheet and pivot charts).
In Sheets, create three different charts from the data. They can be three chart variations of one set, or charts of three different subsets of the data.
Play around with different charts until they create a foundation for a narrative argument/analysis. When you are done take screenshot of the charts, and insert them into a webpage that makes your argument AND that contains the original data -- and yes, you can just take a screenshot of the data and include that in the page itself, although you are welcome to include the core data as an html table. And yes, as a reminder, you can reuse page components from previous labs and assignments.
The completed lab webpage should have:

Header title, clear page focus
Original dataset (as a screenshot or laid out in html tables)
Three charts with accompanying analysis / argument / knowledge. These can be screenshots of the charts.
Every chart must internally have clear data labels.


You can use any platform that creates charts, from google sheets to excel to web-based cahrt generators. Basically anything that will generate a chart from data.
And yes, you could also create the charts directly in the webpage such as with the chartjs javascript library/platform, but that degree of dev is NOT required for this lab... although it would be beneficial for your group if one member started to learn to do so. (We will get into generating charts directly in a page from a javascript library next week.)
