
Potential data areas

This page contains both broad and narrow areas or ideas for data topics/explorations. The first set were prompts/ideas proposed by faculty, when I asked for areas fertile for exploration and data visualizations and simulations. The second set is more generic, and one I listed as potential areas for earlier assignments and labs.

Some good places to browse/search for datasets to explore:

- City and general government data (data.gov, data.lacity.org, etc.)

- govt healthcare data (data.cdc.gov, data.healthcare.gov, etc.)

- kaggle and google dataset search

Specific prompts:

- L.A. Olympics: influx of people versus infrastructure, temporary impact on small biz, restaurants, etc. This could be a comparison to the 1984 olympics to upcoming. How many businesses were around, how many now, what is the GDP difference? Population differences too to see how complex it is. Some negatives that could be explored would be: Number of smog alert days, length of lines at airports, price of hotel rooms, number of traffic incidents, and some positives could include Open Table bookings (if they'd share data), google searches for restaurants, ticket sales at museums, etc.

- Supply chain and product maps: Maps as data viz through supply chains for cars, parts, chips, etc. Could track profits and costs of manufacturers such as the car sector. Correlation in historical data for manufacturers, spikes in supply, etc.? Would have to go very focused on a small category of products. Note that this data is very confidential to individual companies and might be hard to get. And would need to get data from mfg as well as suppliers (who are likely overseas). It would be interesting to see how the Wuhan chip disruption during Covid eventually impacts car sales. Time series analysis on the source of automotive chips and the eventual backlog of sales in the US of cars using those chips. 

- Social trends: How social acceptance of trends impact businesses. Students could potentially compare internal and external practices to see how social trends impact. There is a big wave of performative activism and solidarity amongst corporations that have come under fire in the past.

- Strategic thinking through simulations: For example: go back and say what if the US had kept up with nuclear energy. Look at France (GDP, reliance on foreign oil, use of nuclear, pollution)...back cast and apply those ratios to US growth over the last 30 years and see what our economy and carbon output might have looked like if we had done the same. This can be used then to create a view of where we can get to in the next 30 years if we make this change.

-  Traditional product and commodity connections, i.e. cereal relies of price of wheat, how closely do swings in commodity prices parallel and affect products. Could be price of plastics and oil on battery costs. Etc.

- Pricing influencer budgets based on impact for specific product/product area. Pick influencers (scrape tickptock, other platforms, to pick an influencer to work with, how to scrape the data?) then evaluate engagement rates, view to follower rates, marketing metrics, which is most valuable to product, etc. Based on anticipated conversion rate what would the budget be for the influencer? For different verticals, health product vs tech product vs entertainment-based concept, they all have different cpms.
