Which jobs AI was predicted to touch, and which ones it actually is

We combine a government-style AI exposure rating for 831 US occupations with Anthropic's record of how people really use Claude on each job's tasks, plus how recent grads in 73 majors are doing. A student picking a major or a first job can see where the prediction and the real use agree, and where they split.

Purpose

Most AI and jobs coverage repeats one prediction: a list of exposed occupations. Our site puts three things side by side for each field: what the exposure rating predicts, how much AI is actually being used on that work today, and how graduates heading into it are doing. The goal for the final build is a page where someone enters their field or major and sees all three for it.

Who it is for

Our working assumption is USC students choosing a major or a first job. The M2 interviews test that assumption. [NEED: update after interviews are logged in docs/interviews.md]

Assumptions we are making

  • The exposure rating is relative. The four levels hold 213, 206, 206 and 206 occupations, almost exact quarters, so "Low" means low compared to other jobs, not zero. [NEED: Ellen, the original source of the AIExposure column]
  • Employment numbers are projections. Employment 2025 and 2035 (thousands) and the median wage come from Ellen's dataset, which follows the BLS Employment Projections layout. [NEED: Ellen, confirm the exact source table and year]
  • Observed use is one company's view. Anthropic's score is built from Claude conversations, so it shows how people use one assistant, not all AI tools. It joins to 755 of our 831 occupations by SOC code.
  • The major to field mapping is ours. The NY Fed reports by major; we mapped each major to one of the 21 job fields so the tables join. Three majors have no clean match.
  • Averages are per occupation. "Average projected change" weights a small occupation the same as a large one, which matches our database views.
  • Exposure is not loss. A high rating means AI can do many of a job's tasks. It does not say the job shrinks; the data page tests whether it does.
