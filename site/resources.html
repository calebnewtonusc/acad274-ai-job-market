Where our data and framing come from

The datasets we built the tables from come first, then the research that shaped how we read them. Every link was checked on 2026-11-19.

Datasets we use

  • Dataset, used in Tables 1 and 2

    Anthropic Economic Index dataset (Hugging Face)

    Anthropic's open data release, CC-BY. Our observed AI use column comes from its labor_market_impacts/job_exposure.csv file, a 0 to 1 score per occupation built from how people actually use Claude on that occupation's tasks.

  • Report series

    The Anthropic Economic Index

    Anthropic's ongoing reports on which tasks and occupations people use Claude for, and whether that use automates a task or assists a person doing it.

  • Dataset, used in Table 3

    The Labor Market for Recent College Graduates (Federal Reserve Bank of New York)

    Unemployment, underemployment and early and mid-career wages for 73 majors, for college graduates aged 22 to 27, which is where our recent-grad numbers come from.

  • Government data

    Employment Projections (US Bureau of Labor Statistics)

    BLS's ten-year projections of employment, openings, wages and education requirements by occupation, the format our core occupation table follows. [NEED: Ellen, confirm this is the source of the employment and wage columns]

  • Occupation database

    O*NET OnLine

    The Labor Department's database of tasks, skills and work activities for each occupation, keyed by the same SOC codes as our data, so it is how we could add task-level detail in M3.

Research that shaped our questions

  • Working paper, 2023

    Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin and Rock, "GPTs are GPTs: An Early Look at the Labor Market Impact Potential of Large Language Models"

    Rates occupations by how well their tasks line up with LLM capabilities and finds around 80% of US workers could have at least 10% of their tasks affected, with higher-income jobs facing more exposure.

  • Working paper, 2023

    Felten, Raj and Seamans, "How will Language Modelers like ChatGPT Affect Occupations and Industries?"

    Builds an exposure measure for language AI across occupations, industries and places, and finds telemarketers and many postsecondary teachers most exposed and a positive link between wages and exposure.

  • Working paper, revised August 2026

    Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence"

    Using ADP payroll data through June 2026, finds no economy-wide displacement, but employment of workers aged 22 to 25 in AI-exposed occupations sits 19% below where it would be had it kept pace with less-exposed peers, mostly through less hiring.

  • Survey, 2026

    Gallup and Lumina Foundation, "College Students Weigh AI's Impact on Majors and Careers"

    A survey of 3,801 US college students in October 2025, in which 16% say they have already changed their major or field of study because of AI's potential impact.
